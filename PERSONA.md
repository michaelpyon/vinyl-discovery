# PERSONA.md - CrateDigger (vinyl-discovery)

## The 1 person

**Marcus Tran, 32. Senior data analyst in Chicago. Owns 611 records and counting.**

Marcus travels 4 to 6 times a year, half for work, half for fun, and every trip gets planned around 2 things: food and record stores. He has a Discogs collection meticulously tagged by pressing, a wantlist 300 deep, and a Google Maps layer called "DIG" full of saved pins he half trusts because a third of the shops closed since he pinned them.

## The exact job he hires this for

"I land in Berlin in 3 weeks. Which shops are actually worth my 1 free afternoon, what do they go deep on, and is any of them my kind of store (techno and dub, not indie rock)?"

It is a trip-planning job, not a browsing job. He hires CrateDigger the week before a trip and again on hotel wifi the night before dig day. Secondary job: armchair globe-spinning on a slow Sunday, which is how he found the product in the first place.

## Where he hangs out online

- r/vinyl and r/VinylCollectors on Reddit (posts hauls, asks "shops in Osaka?")
- Discogs forums, specifically the "Record Store Directory" and city-thread corners, plus VinylHub when he remembers it exists
- Vinyl X/Twitter: the #vinylcommunity hashtag orbit and quote-tweeting Bandcamp Daily features
- A small "DIGGERS" group chat of 5 college friends where haul photos and shop tips go first

## What he uses today instead

1. VinylHub / Discogs store directory: comprehensive but ugly, stale, zero taste signal
2. Google Maps saved pins + "best record stores in [city]" blog posts from 2019
3. Reddit city-thread searches, screenshotted into his notes app

None of these tell him what a store digs deep on. That genre fingerprint plus a clearly labeled CrateDigger album pick is the wedge.

## 3 named reference products he considers premium

1. **Radio Garden**: the taste ceiling for this exact interaction. A globe you spin to discover culture by place. He has sent Radio Garden links to the group chat unprompted.
2. **Vinyl Me, Please**: premium curation voice. Staff-pick writing that sounds like a human who owns the record.
3. **Bandcamp (and Bandcamp Daily)**: honest, artist-first, editorial without being precious. The "Curated" label should feel like Bandcamp, not like an SEO listicle.

## His vocabulary

Digging, crates, wantlist, grails, pressings, OG press vs reissue, "deep on jazz", holy grail shops (Hard Wax, Disk Union, Amoeba, A1), "worth the detour", haul. He says "shop" more than "store". He never says "vinyls".

## Device

MacBook Pro on the couch for planning (where the globe should sing), iPhone 15 in the field. Desktop-first is correct, but the store card and share URL must be flawless on mobile because that is what opens in the group chat.

## 5-second bounce triggers

1. Star ratings that look invented or scraped (he cross-checks against shops he has physically visited; 1 fake detail kills all trust)
2. A globe that loads slow or janky, or a blank black screen with no loading state
3. Listings for shops he knows closed (staleness = VinylHub, the thing he already has)
4. Generic AI-template look: purple gradient hero, emoji bullets, "Discover your next favorite record!" copy
5. No way to get from a store card to Discogs or the shop's own site (dead end = toy, not tool)

## The unprompted-share moment

He filters the globe to Techno, the planet strips down to a constellation with Berlin burning brightest, he clicks Hard Wax, the globe flies in, and the card pairs the shop with a clearly labeled CrateDigger album pick. He screen-records the spin-and-fly-in, posts the GIF to the group chat and r/vinyl with "found the shop map I always wanted", and pastes the per-store share URL. The share URL resolving to that exact card is what converts his 5 friends.

## V2 evangelist verdict

The card's prior ("32-year-old collector on r/vinyl and Discogs forums") is directionally right and I am keeping it, but sharpened: the winning frame is the **traveling** collector planning a dig day, not the at-home browser. Trip planning is the job with real urgency, an existing bad workflow (blog posts + stale pins), and a natural share loop (city tips are the top recurring ask in every vinyl community). Do not drift toward casual music fans; they bounce off a 37-store dataset, while Marcus reads 37 real, correct shops as credibility.
